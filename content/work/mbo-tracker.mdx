## Overview

Every team in the sales org tracked MBOs differently, in PDFs, Excel sheets and Word docs. I designed and built MBO Tracker, a desktop app that puts objectives, payout progress and AI coaching in one place.

My team used the first version. Then a designer told me it looked "very AI-generated." She was right, so I audited it and redesigned every screen in Figma with IBM's Carbon Design System. This case study covers both versions and why each change was made.

## The problem

- **Sellers** switched between files to answer "Where am I?" and "What will I earn?"
- **Managers** spent one-on-ones rebuilding status instead of coaching.
- **Compensation data** is sensitive, so it had to stay on the user's machine.

## Version 1: designed in code

I designed directly in code with IBM Bob, so my team tested a working product, not a prototype. It had a dashboard, a table, a Kanban board, spreadsheet import with AI column matching, and coaching from IBM Granite running on the user's own computer.

I tested it with my team of 12, including my manager. One teammate installed Ollama but got stuck downloading the model, because Ollama's site doesn't explain that step. I made it its own step with the exact command to copy.

## Why I redesigned it

In a design review, a designer said the app looked "very very AI-generated." The app already used Carbon components and IBM Plex, so Carbon wasn't the problem. The problem was a layer of decoration on top of it, made of patterns that AI tools reach for by default:

- An uppercase label above every section ("OBJECTIVES AT A GLANCE", "PROGRESS BREAKDOWN").
- Four equal stat cards, each number in a different color.
- Boxes inside boxes inside boxes.
- Colored accent borders, rounded pills and hover shadows on a system that is meant to be square and flat.
- A donut chart where one slice was 93% of the whole.
- Purple for "Exceeded", blue for four unrelated things, and yellow and orange text that failed contrast.

Looks were only half of it. The audit also found problems that misled people:

- **The completion bars lied.** Each bar was scaled to the largest value, so 14% could fill the whole track.
- **The first launch said you'd failed.** With no objectives yet, the dashboard showed "Tier 5, 0%, Did not meet KPIs" in red.
- **Two percentages sat side by side.** "Tier 3, 80%" was the payout. "81%" was average completion. Neither was labeled.
- **Nothing answered "what next?"** Finding what was behind meant filtering the table or scanning the board.
- **The AI summary was wrong.** It said "reaching Tier 5 will be within your grasp", but Tier 5 is the lowest tier, and the claim appeared with no label saying AI wrote it.

## What guided version 2

1. **Answer one question first.** The main screen exists to answer "What will I earn, and what do I do next?" Everything else moves down or onto its own page.
2. **One color, one meaning.** Blue is for actions and progress. Green, yellow and red are only for status, always with an icon and a label so color is never the only signal.
3. **AI suggests, the app counts.** The app calculates every number. Granite only writes the sentences around them, and it's labeled as AI everywhere it appears.
4. **Stock Carbon, used properly.** Square corners, one layer of containers and sentence-case headings. The design system does the work, so nothing needs decorating.

**Why:** Version 1 opened with how many objectives were in each status. That's the least useful number for a seller. What they actually want to know is their payout and how to improve it.

**What changed:**
- The projected tier is the largest text on the screen, beside a scale of every tier. You can see how far you are from the next one.
- Under it, one sentence turns the gap into a plan: "22.3 points to Tier 3. Finishing any 4 of the 5 open ones below gets you there."
- **Needs attention** lists the open objectives ranked by how many points each would add to your average.
- The four stat cards and the donut became one small "This window" breakdown. Progress bars now use a fixed 0 to 125% scale with a marker at 100%.

**How it helps:** The weekly check-in goes from reading four cards, a donut and a banner to reading one number and a short ranked list.

**Why:** Every row repeated its category, window and status as colored tags. The Deadline column was empty for every row. The Kanban board split "Complete" and "Exceeded" into separate columns for the same outcome.

**What changed:**

**How it helps:** Scanning 30 objectives no longer means reading 90 tags, and it's clear which objectives count toward the payout right now.

## 7. Coaching you can check

<Compare
  before={{ src: "/images/work/mbo-tracker/14-ai-coaching-summary.webp", width: 2400, height: 562, alt: "Version 1 AI performance summary paragraph" }}
  after={{ src: "/images/work/mbo-tracker/v2-05-coaching.webp", width: 2400, height: 1340, alt: "Version 2 Coaching page with three labeled suggestions linked to objectives, earlier suggestions, and a panel explaining how suggestions work" }}
  caption="Three specific suggestions with links, an AI label, and a clear note on where the numbers come from."

**Why:** The old summary was seven paragraphs of encouragement, one of which got the tier system backwards.

**What changed:**
- Coaching is now three suggestions a week, each tied to a real objective with "Open" and "Mark as done" links.
- Each set is labeled AI and says when it was written and from what data.
- A side panel explains that the app calculates every number, that Granite only writes the sentences, and that nothing leaves the computer.
- Earlier weeks show how many suggestions were acted on.

**How it helps:** Every claim can be traced to a number the app calculated, so an AI mistake is easy to catch instead of easy to trust.

## Iterating on the redesign

The first Figma pass used a Carbon side nav. Once the screens were in, the side nav left a tall empty column beside every page, so I moved navigation into the top header. That gave tables and charts about 250px more width.

Then I critiqued my own redesign as a reviewer would, and ranked what I found:

1. **"50%" still meant two things.** The tier read "Tier 4, 50% payout" and the scale below it read "Tier 4, 50%", where 50% was the completion needed. The scale now says "from 50%", "from 80%" and so on.
2. **The tier wasn't the first thing you saw.** It was the same size as the page title, and two buttons pulled the eye away. I enlarged the tier, cut the page down to one primary action, and stretched the scale to fill its panel.
3. **"+6.3 pts" had no explanation.** The list now says the points are what finishing each objective adds to your average.
4. **The search placeholder failed contrast,** at 2.16:1 in the light theme and 2.30:1 in the dark one. It's now 4.57:1 and 4.86:1, above the WCAG AA minimum.
5. **Click targets were too small.** The modal close control was an 18px-tall text link and radio buttons were 18px tall. The close control is now a 48px button, and radio buttons are 24px.

Smaller fixes evened out padding, table row heights and page spacing.

## The system behind it

All 21 frames are built from Figma variables for Carbon's Gray 10 and Gray 100 themes, plus a spacing scale and Carbon's productive type scale in IBM Plex Sans. Components such as status indicators, progress bars, fields, notifications and table rows are shared, so dark mode is a mode switch, not a second design.
