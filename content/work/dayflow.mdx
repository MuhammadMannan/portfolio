## Overview

DayFlow is my own Flutter and Firebase planner. It worked, but only just: tasks had no dates, so anything unfinished disappeared at midnight. I audited the app, redesigned it in Figma around a dated task model, streaks and the phone's calendar, then rebuilt it in Flutter.

## The problem

Behind the sign-in screens, the original app was one working page: a list of tasks created today.

- **Tasks had no date.** Home only showed tasks whose creation date was today. Miss one and it vanished from every screen, though it stayed in the database.
- **Nothing to plan with.** No due dates, times, reminders or repeats, so a calendar or streak couldn't be built on top.
- **Navigation that wasn't tabs.** Each tab pushed a new page, the bar looked different on every screen, and the Tasks tab was an empty page with a hard-coded date.
- **Two visual styles.** Sign-in was black and white; everything after it was a hand-built blue theme.

## Audit

I walked every screen on the iPhone simulator and read all 2,099 lines of Dart to confirm how each one behaves. Besides the missing dates, I found that adding a task without a category crashed, a completed task disappeared with no undo, sign out sat one tap away in the top corner with no confirmation, and the dark theme was defined but never used.

Every planned feature, from streaks to the calendar to reminders, depended on one change: tasks needed a due date, an optional time and a completion date. That became the first decision, and the rest of the design follows from it.

## Decisions before pixels

I wrote down the rules the design would assume, so each screen had something to answer to:

1. **A streak day is one completed task.** "Finish everything you planned" punishes ambitious planning. A daily goal of 1, 3 or 5 is optional, and one streak freeze a week is used automatically.
2. **One tag per task.** Several tags would make the "what did I spend time on" breakdown double-count.
3. **Calendar is read-only.** Events come from the phone's own calendars (iCloud, Google, Outlook) through EventKit, so there's no separate sign-in for each.
4. **Nothing disappears at midnight.** Unfinished tasks move to Overdue until they're done, rescheduled or dismissed.

## 1. Home answers "what's my day?"

A greeting, the week strip and a progress ring with the last seven days replace the percentage and date tiles. Below them, today's tasks and the next few days. The streak chip and avatar move into the header, and sign out moves into Profile.

<Figure src="/images/work/dayflow/03-home.webp" width={2400} height={1760} alt="Before and after of the Home screen" caption="The ring says how much is left today and whether the streak is safe. Coming up shows what's next, with tag colours carried through." />

## 2. Add a task in one line

The centre + is in every tab. Quick add only needs a title, and it reads dates and times out of what you type: "Call mom tomorrow 6pm" becomes a task with a date, time and reminder. Repeat, notes and tag sit one tap further.

<Figure src="/images/work/dayflow/04-add.webp" width={2400} height={1760} alt="Before and after of adding a task: the old three-field form and the new quick add sheet" caption="The old form had no date at all. The new sheet shows what it picked up, so it can be corrected before saving." />

## 3. A Tasks tab that holds everything

Tasks is now the full list: Overdue, Today, Upcoming grouped by day, Someday for undated ideas, and Completed. Tag filters sit at the top, and a quick add field means you never leave the list to capture something.

<Figure src="/images/work/dayflow/05-tasks.webp" width={2400} height={1760} alt="Before and after of the Tasks tab" caption="An empty page with a hard-coded date became the place every task lives." />

## 4. Tasks and events, side by side

The month view puts a dot on each day with tasks or events and shows the chosen day underneath. The week view lays the day out hour by hour, so a 4 PM task sits next to the meeting before it. Events from the phone's calendars are marked read-only.

<Figure src="/images/work/dayflow/06-calendar.webp" width={2400} height={1760} alt="Calendar month view and week timeline view" caption="Tapping an empty day or time slot starts a task already dated to it." />

## 5. Onboarding that earns its permissions

Three short screens, each skippable: what counts as a good day, why DayFlow wants your calendar, and what reminders will send. Each iOS permission prompt comes only after the screen that explains it.

<Figure src="/images/work/dayflow/07-onboarding.webp" width={2400} height={1500} alt="Onboarding screens for the daily goal, calendar access and reminders" caption="The calendar primer states up front that DayFlow never edits or deletes events." />

## 6. Streaks that forgive a bad day

Completing a task gives a haptic, a check animation and an undo toast. The first completion of the day extends the streak, and 7, 30 and 100 days get a milestone screen. If nothing is done by 8 PM, one optional nudge goes out.

## One system, light and dark

I kept the DayFlow name and its blue and replaced both old styles with one set of tokens in Figma variables, with Light and Dark modes. Type is Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. The tag colours were re-picked after the first set failed a colour-vision check.

## From Figma to Flutter

I built the redesign straight from the Figma file: the Flutter theme uses the same token names as the Figma variables, and tasks moved to a new dated model in Firestore keyed by user id rather than email. Reminders, the streak nudge, onboarding, the completion animation, milestones and the week view are working in the app today.

## What's next

- **Test it** with a handful of people over a week, since streaks only prove themselves over days.

- **Widgets.** A streak app lives on being seen every day, so the home and lock screen widgets in the file are next after that.
